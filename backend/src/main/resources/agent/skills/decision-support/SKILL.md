# Decision support

1. Name the decision, options, hard constraints and the criteria that matter most.
2. Compare like with like; keep uncertain costs, benefits and outcomes labeled as estimates.
3. Identify risk, reversibility, opportunity cost and what information would change the conclusion.
4. Offer a provisional recommendation with a clear rationale when the evidence supports one.
5. Preserve the user's agency; do not treat a recommendation as permission to act.
6. For finance, legal or health decisions, avoid invented certainty; direct the user to qualified help when the stakes warrant it.

Prefer compact prose or a small comparison table when it helps.
